Fragments of Grace

Chapter One:
The Room Where Everything Felt Heavy

“The Lord is near unto them that are of a broken heart.”
Psalm 34:18

The house had never felt this quiet. Silence rested heavily within every room, pressing against the walls and settling deep into the air until even breathing felt difficult. Bradley sat motionless in his worn armchair with the muted television glowing faintly across the room, pretending to watch while his thoughts drifted somewhere far beyond the screen. Nothing inside the house felt normal anymore. Every room carried a strange pressure that none of them fully understood, yet all of them felt.

Ashley remained curled into the corner of the couch beneath a blanket, staring absentmindedly at a cup of coffee that had long since gone cold. Exhaustion rested visibly across her face despite her attempts to hide it behind loose strands of dark hair. She had always been the strong one among them, steady through every crisis and dependable during every storm, though something inside her had changed over the past several months. Bradley noticed the difference more each day. The warmth and confidence that once defined her had slowly faded beneath a quiet sadness she could neither explain nor escape.

Cassandra drifted silently through the hallway nearby, moving carefully as though trying not to disturb something unseen. Her phone buzzed constantly throughout the day, yet every conversation happened behind partially closed doors and in hushed whispers. She avoided eye contact more often now. Small details betrayed her no matter how hard she tried appearing composed. Her hands trembled occasionally. Dark circles deepened beneath her eyes. Some nights she barely slept at all.

Bradley recognized every sign.

The sleepless nights. The excuses. The growing distance between words and truth. Whatever weighed upon the house had already begun weighing upon all three of them in different ways.

Ashley finally broke the silence first.

“Do you ever feel like something is wrong here?” she asked quietly without looking up.

Bradley turned his attention toward her, though he answered slowly. “What do you mean?”

Her fingers tightened around the coffee cup.

“I do not know how to explain it.” She hesitated before continuing. “Some mornings I wake up feeling completely empty. Other mornings feel heavy before my feet even touch the floor. Not sadness exactly. Not fear either. Just... pressure. Like something sits on my chest all day.”

The honesty in her voice unsettled him more than the words themselves.

Ashley lowered her eyes again. “Sometimes I feel watched.”

Bradley remained silent for several seconds.

He understood exactly what she meant.

For weeks he had experienced the same uneasiness after midnight whenever the house settled into darkness. More than once he woke suddenly with the overwhelming sense that somebody stood near his doorway. Prayer had also become strangely difficult. Even peaceful moments inside the house never truly felt peaceful anymore, as though tension lingered invisibly beneath everything.

“You are not the only one,” he admitted carefully.

Ashley looked toward him almost immediately, relief and fear mixing together within her expression. “So you feel it too?”

Before Bradley could answer, Cassandra stepped into the living room. Smudged makeup rested beneath tired eyes that revealed far more than she intended. She leaned quietly against the doorway for a moment while studying both of them with caution.

“What are you talking about?”

Ashley hesitated briefly before answering.

“The heaviness in this house.”

The reaction across Cassandra’s face came instantly. Her posture stiffened and the color drained from her expression.

“I do not want to talk about that,” she muttered under her breath.

Ashley frowned gently. “Why not?”

Cassandra swallowed hard before answering. “Because sometimes I hear things.”

The room fell completely still.

“Thoughts that do not feel like mine,” Cassandra continued quietly. “Sometimes words just appear inside my head out of nowhere, and no matter how hard I try praying, they keep coming back.”

Ashley stared at her in stunned silence before whispering, “That has been happening to me too.”

The remote slipped from Bradley’s hand and struck the floor with a dull sound that echoed sharply through the room. None of them moved afterward. A cold stillness slowly settled over the house again, subtle enough to seem imaginary yet strong enough that all three of them felt it immediately.

“There,” Ashley whispered nervously. “That feeling again.”

Bradley rose slowly from the chair while looking between both sisters. Fear lingered within the room, though another realization now stood beside it. Whatever this darkness was, none of them were facing it alone anymore.

“This feels spiritual,” he said carefully.

Neither sister argued.

A soft creak echoed somewhere deeper within the hallway moments later, followed by slow measured footsteps that did not belong to any of them. Cassandra immediately wrapped her arms around herself while Ashley gripped the edge of the couch.

“Did you hear that?” Cassandra asked quietly.

Bradley stepped closer to both of them, keeping his voice steady despite the tension pressing inward around the room.

“There is one name darkness fears,” he said. “Jesus. The one true God revealed in flesh. No spirit has authority above His name.”

The atmosphere remained tense for another lingering moment before the heaviness finally eased enough for all three of them to breathe normally again. Peace had not fully returned, though something important had changed. Fear no longer controlled the room the same way it had before.

Bradley rested his hand against the back of the couch and looked directly at his sisters.

“No more hiding from this,” he said firmly. “Tomorrow we talk openly about everything.”

Ashley nodded quietly while Cassandra lowered her eyes.

“I am scared,” she admitted softly.

Bradley held her gaze without hesitation.

“Then Jesus is about to remind this house who He is.”

Silence returned once more, though the silence felt different now. The heaviness remained somewhere within the house, watching and waiting from unseen places, yet another presence now stood against it. Something hidden had finally been acknowledged, and whatever darkness lingered there knew the silence had been broken.
