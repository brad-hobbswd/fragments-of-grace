Fragments of Grace

Chapter Two:
Uninvited Conversations

“For we wrestle not against flesh and blood, but against principalities, against powers.”
Ephesians 6:12

Morning light slipped weakly through the blinds, though the house still felt darker than it should have. A strange heaviness lingered within every room, subtle enough to avoid explanation yet impossible to ignore once noticed. Bradley stood quietly in the kitchen waiting for the coffee to finish brewing while listening to the silence surrounding him. Silence used to feel peaceful inside the house. Recently silence felt aware, almost like something listening from somewhere just beyond sight.

Ashley entered the kitchen slowly with exhaustion written clearly across her face. Another sleepless night had deepened the shadows beneath her eyes, and her movements carried the drained caution of someone already overwhelmed before the day had fully begun. Cassandra appeared moments later wearing an oversized sweater with her hands wrapped tightly around an empty coffee mug. Though neither sister immediately spoke, Bradley could see the same tension resting across both of them.

Nobody needed to explain what they felt anymore.

The pressure surrounding the house had become too obvious to deny.

Bradley finally broke the silence while pouring coffee into three mugs.

“We need to talk openly about this.”

Ashley exchanged a quick glance with Cassandra, though neither objected. Fear still lingered between them, yet avoiding the subject no longer felt possible after everything they experienced during the previous night.

The three eventually gathered in the living room again, settling almost instinctively into the same places they occupied before. The room itself seemed unsettled somehow, carrying the memory of what had already been spoken there.

Ashley pulled her knees close beneath the blanket wrapped around her shoulders before quietly saying, “I woke up twice last night because I felt like somebody was standing beside my bed.”

Bradley nodded slowly. “That has happened to me too.”

Cassandra lowered her eyes toward the floor for several moments before finally speaking. “Mine feels worse lately,” she admitted quietly. “Sometimes thoughts just appear in my head out of nowhere. They tell me I am broken beyond repair. They tell me God could never want somebody like me after everything I struggle with.”

Ashley’s expression immediately tightened with emotion.

“I hear those same thoughts,” she whispered. “Sometimes they arrive so suddenly that I start believing them before realizing where they came from.”

Bradley leaned forward slightly, resting his forearms across his knees while listening carefully.

“I experience the same attacks,” he admitted. “Especially late at night when everything becomes quiet. The thoughts feel invasive, almost like they push inward before I even recognize them.”

Cassandra slowly nodded.

“That is exactly what scares me,” she said softly. “Sometimes the thoughts do not even feel like mine.”

The moment the words left her mouth, a sharp clicking sound echoed from somewhere inside the hallway. Every head turned immediately toward the darkness beyond the living room entrance. A second later came the unmistakable sound of slow measured footsteps crossing the floor.

Nobody stood there.

Yet the atmosphere shifted instantly.

Ashley’s voice dropped to barely above a whisper. “Do you feel that?”

Bradley nodded carefully while standing from his chair. The air near the hallway felt noticeably heavier, charged with a strange spiritual tension that tightened across his chest the closer he moved toward it.

“This is not insanity,” he said calmly while turning back toward his sisters. “This is spiritual influence. Darkness works quietly. Fear, exhaustion, shame, isolation, hopelessness. Those things create openings where lies begin sounding believable.”

Ashley rubbed her temples slowly. “Why would darkness target us like this?”

Bradley answered without hesitation.

“Because purpose attracts resistance. The enemy fights hardest wherever healing, restoration, or calling threatens to break through.”

Cassandra’s voice trembled slightly. “Whenever I try praying lately, my mind feels blocked.”

“That is why secrecy becomes dangerous,” Bradley replied. “Darkness survives where silence hides everything.”

Ashley glanced toward the old bookshelf near the corner of the room where several framed Scriptures still rested untouched from years earlier. After a moment she stood and carefully picked one up, reading the familiar words aloud.

“For we wrestle not against flesh and blood.”

The atmosphere inside the room tightened almost immediately.

Cassandra inhaled sharply while gripping the couch cushion beside her.

“Something does not want us reading that.”

Bradley stepped closer, gently taking the frame before opening the worn Bible resting nearby. His voice remained calm and steady despite the pressure building around them.

“Put on the whole armor of God, that ye may be able to stand against the wiles of the devil.”

The lamp beside the couch flickered once.

Ashley stared toward it with widened eyes. “Keep reading.”

Bradley continued reading from Ephesians chapter six while the room around them seemed to shift uneasily beneath the sound of Scripture.

“For we wrestle not against flesh and blood, but against principalities, against powers, against the rulers of the darkness of this world, against spiritual wickedness in high places.”

The heaviness surrounding the house reacted almost strangely afterward, not violently but like something slowly retreating beneath truth it could no longer comfortably remain around.

Cassandra looked toward Bradley with visible shock.

“It is moving.”

Ashley rubbed her arms as chills passed through her body.

“It hates hearing Scripture.”

Bradley slowly closed the Bible before looking toward both sisters with calm certainty resting across his expression.

“That tells us exactly what this battle is,” he said quietly. “And more importantly, that tells us who already holds authority over it.”

The pressure within the room eased noticeably afterward. Fear still lingered, though hopelessness no longer dominated the atmosphere the same way it had before.

Ashley wiped quietly beneath her eyes before asking, “What do we do now?”

Bradley looked once more toward the hallway where the heaviness still lingered faintly in the background.

“We stop hiding. We pray openly. We expose every lie. And we speak the name of Jesus over this house until darkness understands it no longer belongs here.”

Cassandra nodded slowly while holding her coffee mug tighter between both hands.

“I want help,” she admitted softly. “I do not want to fall deeper into this.”

Bradley’s expression softened.

“You are not alone,” he said firmly. “Jesus is already stepping into this battle with us.”

Silence eventually settled across the house again, though this silence no longer felt entirely oppressive. Something had shifted during the conversation. Fear had been confronted directly, truth had been spoken aloud, and the darkness lingering quietly around them now understood the family was no longer ignoring its presence.
